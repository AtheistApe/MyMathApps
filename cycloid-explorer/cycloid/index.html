    Questions for students
    • Where is the dot at θ = π? At θ = 2π? Answer: (πr, 2r), the top of the arch, and (2πr, 0), back on the ground.
    • For θ between π/2 and 3π/2, r cos θ is negative. Does the formula for y still work? Answer: yes. The dot is above the hub, and subtracting a negative adds.
    • Turn on the velocity vector. Where is the dot moving fastest, and where does it stop? Answer: fastest at the top, at twice the wheel's speed. It is momentarily at rest when it touches the ground.
    • At the cusps both dx/dθ and dy/dθ are 0. Is the curve smooth there? Answer: no. The parametrization is smooth, but the curve has a cusp, and dy/dx → ±∞.
    • Set d < r. Why do the cusps disappear? Set d > r. Why does the curve form loops? Answer: dx/dθ = r − d cos θ. It is always positive when d < r, and it turns negative near θ = 2πk when d > r.
    Facts about one arch (0 ≤ θ ≤ 2π, d = r)
    • Arc length = 8r, which is exactly 4 times the wheel's diameter. Compare with the "arc length traced" readout.
    • Area under one arch = 3πr², which is 3 times the area of the wheel.
    • Speed: |dP/dθ| = 2r|sin(θ/2)|.
    • An upside-down cycloid is the brachistochrone (the fastest slide between two points) and the tautochrone (a bead reaches the bottom in the same time from any starting point).
